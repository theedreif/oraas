# ORaaS Validation Ladder

## Premise

Synthetic performance is not automatically field readiness.

A polished dashboard can reproduce the same mistake ORaaS is designed to replace if loop scores are treated as proof without demonstrating transfer.

The manuscript therefore defines a five-rung evidence ladder.

## Rung 1 — Loop Evidence

Capture what the operator actually does in the synthetic environment.

This establishes that the system can observe and score behavior inside the loop.

It is the floor.

It is not yet proof of field readiness.

## Rung 2 — Supervisor Calibration

Put a real subject-matter expert beside the telemetry.

Ask:

> Does the score match the expert's judgment of the operator's behavior?

When expert judgment and the system disagree during calibration, investigate and repair the system rather than assuming the machine is right.

## Rung 3 — Near-Miss Comparison

Compare the failure patterns generated by the loop with recent:

- incidents;
- near misses;
- escalations;
- post-mortems;
- quality escapes;
- operational exceptions.

Ask whether the synthetic system detects patterns that actually occur in the field.

## Rung 4 — Field Outcome Correlation

Track the downstream outcome that matters in the real environment.

Depending on the domain, examples may include:

- time to intervention;
- escalation frequency;
- rework;
- handoff quality;
- incident rate;
- deployment delay;
- customer retention.

Do not substitute a convenient learning metric for the operational outcome.

## Rung 5 — Validated Readiness Claim

Only after the synthetic signal has demonstrated a meaningful relationship with field outcomes should the organization make a readiness claim based on it.

Until then:

> **The Heat Map is a development instrument with an open transfer-validity question.**

## Rule

**Do not claim readiness because the loop looks predictive. Earn the claim in arrears.**

A buyer should be able to distinguish clearly among:

- measured synthetic behavior;
- expert-calibrated behavior;
- patterns consistent with field evidence;
- outcomes correlated with the loop;
- a validated readiness claim.
